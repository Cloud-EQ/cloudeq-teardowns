## Overview

Harbor & Pine had a strong fiscal 2025. Revenue rose 12% to $4.20 billion, driven almost entirely by e-commerce, while stores grew slowly and wholesale was flat. Profit grew faster than sales because online orders carried better margins and the company trimmed discounting.

## Revenue

E-commerce was the story of the year, growing about 32% and now making up 38% of sales, up from 32% a year earlier.

## Profitability

Gross margin improved 1.5 points to 42.5%, helped by a richer mix of private-label products and fewer markdowns. Operating income reached about $550 million, a 13.1% margin versus 12.0% the year before.

## Cash flow and balance sheet

Free cash flow climbed to $365 million from $280 million as earnings grew and inventory stayed under control. The company ended the year with $520 million of cash against $640 million of debt, a net debt position of $120 million.

## Takeaways

- Growth is now online-led. Stores are steady but no longer the engine.
- Margin gains came from mix and pricing discipline, not cost cutting, which makes them more durable.
- Wholesale being flat is worth watching, since it is the segment most exposed to retailer inventory cycles.
- Next quarter: whether e-commerce can keep growing above 25% against tougher comparisons.
